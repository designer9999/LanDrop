# LanDrop 2.0.0 — conversations, redesigned

LanDrop now works like a messenger between your devices. Messages and files are
one conversation, every message has its own menu, and media opens at full
resolution. Devices running 1.8.x can still exchange text and files with 2.0;
the new conversation features turn on automatically when both sides run 2.0.

## Conversations

- **Edit, delete and reply.** Right-click (or long-press) any message, or use the
  hover buttons. Edit your own messages (↑ edits the last one), delete them for
  yourself or for everyone, and reply with a quote that jumps to the original.
  Edited messages show "edited"; deleted ones leave a short placeholder.
- **Reactions.** Six quick reactions on every message, shown on both devices.
- **Delivery state.** A clock while sending, one check when sent, two when the other
  device confirms it received and saved the message. Failed messages stay in the
  conversation with **Retry** instead of returning to the composer.
- **Text and files together.** A caption and its attachments travel as one message.
- **Rich text.** Links open in your browser; `inline code` and fenced code blocks
  are formatted with syntax colours and a Copy button.
- **Drafts per conversation.** Switching devices keeps each draft with its own
  conversation. Notifications open the right conversation without asking you to
  discard anything.
- **Readable history.** Conversations with offline devices stay open and searchable
  under **Earlier**; unread badges and a "new messages" divider show what arrived.

## Media and files

- Paste any number of screenshots, images or videos, or files copied in Explorer.
- Images and videos load straight from disk through Tauri's asset protocol, with
  cached thumbnails, instead of being copied through the app as byte arrays.
  Videos stream with seeking instead of loading whole into memory.
- A new viewer shows full-resolution images with zoom and pan, plays videos, and
  steps through every photo and video in the conversation with ← and →. Copy an
  image to the clipboard, open it, or show it in its folder.
- Each transfer shows its own progress, speed and a **Cancel** button inside the
  message, so several transfers no longer share one progress bar.

## Layout

- Wide windows show a device list beside the conversation; narrow windows keep the
  compact device strip and open the list from the menu button.
- Material 3 Expressive bubbles, avatars that change shape when selected, date
  separators, sticky day labels, a jump-to-latest button with a new-message count,
  and Ctrl+F search inside a conversation.

## Reliability and security fixes

- A file name such as `🎉:party.png` from another device no longer crashes the
  receive session. Names keep their real characters (`photo #1.jpg`, `.gitignore`,
  emoji); only characters no platform can store are replaced, and Windows reserved
  names such as `CON` are prefixed.
- The TCP listener recovers after network changes instead of retrying a rebind that
  could never succeed.
- Files that arrived before a batch failed now appear in the conversation, marked
  as incomplete.
- Receive-folder settings and the device name are written atomically.
- Text previews read at most 256 KiB instead of the whole file.
- Chat links open only `http`/`https` addresses; the webview no longer holds the
  general opener permissions.
- Pasted images are stored under unpredictable names in the app's own cache.

## Compatibility

The wire protocol keeps its framing and port (TCP 29171). New fields are optional
and omitted when unused, so 1.8.x devices parse every 2.0 frame they receive.
Edits, deletions and reactions are only sent to devices that advertise support;
with a 1.8.x device they apply on your side and LanDrop says so. Saved history from
1.8.x loads unchanged.

The transfer protocol is still unencrypted and does not authenticate devices. Use
LanDrop on networks and with devices you trust; see [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md).

## Updated components

Tauri 2.12 with all plugins, Svelte 5.57, Vite 8.3, Vitest 5.0, ESLint 10.13,
TypeScript ESLint 8.72, Rust 1.99.1 and Node 24.22. TypeScript stays on 6.0 until
the Svelte checker and the ESLint TypeScript plugin support TypeScript 7.
